Bored?

Privacy Policy

Last updated November 22, 2026.

The short version: Bored stores an anonymous account, encrypted things it can't read, and the posts and feedback you make public.

On Bored's server

  • An anonymous account: a random ID, the public keys other people use to encrypt things for you, and, if you use a passkey, its public half (never the passkey itself).
  • Your vault: your contacts, the nicknames and tags you give them, your posts, and who you've shared them with. It's encrypted in your browser before it's sent, so Bored can't read it.
  • Encrypted mail: private posts, requests to join, answers, and chats. The server can see which inbox each message goes to, when, and how big it is, but not what it says or who sent it.
  • Your public posts. Anyone can read them, which is the point.
  • Who you've connected with through an invitation. This isn't encrypted.
  • Feedback you post, and your replies to feedback. This isn't encrypted, so please don't put anything personal in it. It's filed under your account's random ID, so you can delete it, and deleting a post also deletes its replies. Deleted feedback is gone from the server's disk after its next restart.
  • Your device's push address, if you turn on notifications.

What Bored doesn't keep

  • Your IP address, or any log of what you do. Like every website, Bored's server sees your IP address with each request, but it doesn't write it down.
  • Who searched for which place. Place searches go through Bored's server to OpenStreetMap, so your browser doesn't have to contact them, and nothing about you is passed along.

In your browser

  • Two cookies: one that keeps you signed in (for 30 days, deleted when you sign out), and one that remembers you've seen the introduction page (it holds nothing about you).
  • Your keys, locked in the browser's storage so that not even the app can copy them out. Signing out deletes them.
  • Your preferences: theme, currency, and Discover filters, including the place you search near. These never leave your device, but they stay on it after you sign out.

That's everything. There are no analytics, ads, or trackers, so there's no cookie banner: there's nothing to agree to.

Who else sees anything

  • OpenStreetMap, while you have the map open to pick a place: your browser loads the map from them, so they see your IP address and the area you're looking at.
  • Everyone signed in to Bored can read all feedback and its replies. Each post and reply shows its author's profile icon, but never their account ID. People you've connected with also see the name they gave you; nobody else sees a name. Developers can mark feedback as done or won't do, and how much of yours is marked done shows as a Tester badge on your own Profile page.
  • Your browser's push service (Google, Apple, Mozilla, or Microsoft), if you turn on notifications. It delivers an encrypted “New activity” ping that it can't read.

For how the encryption works, see the encryption spec. Questions? Send feedback.
